Quick Primer: Key Modeling & Fusion Terms (No Physics Background Needed)
Modelica (.mo) & ORNL TRANSFORM A standard equation-based language engineers use to wire virtual power plants (pipes, valves, heat exchangers). TRANSFORM is Oak Ridge National Laboratory's open-source library for nuclear & fusion thermal systems.
Stiff DAE (Differential-Algebraic System) A model mixing ultra-fast reactions (microseconds) with slow thermal drift (minutes) plus algebraic constraints (e.g., pressure balances) that must hold at every instant.
PbLi & FLiBe Coolants PbLi (liquid lead-lithium metal) absorbs fusion heat and breeds tritium fuel inside the blanket. FLiBe (molten salt) carries heat from the heat exchanger to the electricity turbine.
Differentiable Physics (Adjoints / jax.grad) Running backpropagation through the entire physical simulation so optimizers get exact slopes ("how does turbine power change if I tweak coolant flow?") in one backward pass instead of thousands of guesses.

Why Direct GPU Export Fails:

Bottleneck 1 · Serial CPU Execution

Traditional Modelica tools (OpenModelica omc, Dymola) compile to sequential C code running one trajectory at a time (~0.25–5 s per run). Sweeping 1,000 off-design uncertainties or training a 1,000,000-episode RL policy would take days to weeks on a CPU.

Bottleneck 2 · Black-Box No-Gradient Optimization

Legacy simulators are opaque black boxes: optimizing 6+ coupled plant variables or neural controller weights requires slow derivative-free search. Putting physics inside JAX enables both 1,000×–100,000× GPU batching (vmap) and exact backpropagation (jax.grad).

Empirical Caveats From Phase 3–4

  • Shared-GPU XLA Lock Contention: Sharing one GPU across 3 concurrent swarms under a 60-min wall limit stalled peers during 200–500 s XLA compiles. Moving to dedicated GPUs (--gres=gpu:3) eliminates this cleanly.
  • Small Per-Gen Sample Size: 2+1 swarms/gen showed strong pass-rate lifts (0/3 → 3/3 on Tier 3, 6/6 on Tier 2) but carries variance (higher chatter in Tier 3 Gen-2 lessons yielded conservative step counts vs. control). Larger populations (4+2) will tighten statistical bounds.
